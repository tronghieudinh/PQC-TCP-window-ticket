WP6 part F (pre-registered). Question: what in the state of the server's sender decides whether an acknowledgment of the session ticket grows the window, and by how much?
ticket_state.bt logs, at every ACK processed by the server's Reno (tcp_reno_cong_avoid), snd_cwnd, ssthresh, packets_out, max_packets_out, is_cwnd_limited, and snd_cwnd after tcp_slow_start.
Events are joined with the server-side capture by client port and acknowledgment number. Criteria (analyze_state.py):
P1  tcp_slow_start is called iff (is_cwnd_limited or snd_cwnd < 2 * max_packets_out) at entry: >= 98 % of the ticket-ACK events. A lower share means that the logged state is not what the kernel tests.
P2  The first burst of the response equals (snd_cwnd after the last ticket ACK that arrived before the write) - (ticket segments still unacknowledged at the write), within one segment: >= 90 % of the transactions.
P3  The ACKs within 0.1 ms of the 0.049 ms threshold of part C (the part D misses, 14 of 288) agree with the state-based prediction for >= 90 % of the events: the threshold rule is a proxy for the state.
Exploratory (no criterion): whether is_cwnd_limited or max_packets_out is the variable that differs between the ACKs before and after the last request segment.
If P1 holds and P2 fails, the growth of the window follows another path than slow start at the ticket's ACKs (for instance the ACKs of the response); if P3 fails, the 14 misses are not a clock artefact.
