# Round 3, step 1: exploration of the archived race traces (22 Nov 2026; exploratory, no criterion; script `r3_explore.py`, points in `r3_explore_points.csv`)

Written for: the authors. Data: the clock-safe repeats and the exploratory runs r2x and r2y (X250, Optiplex, GX10; race design, request 19 460 or 25 000 B, 100 Mbit/s, RTT 10 to 100 ms): 1 999 resumed transactions. For each: n ticket segments, k_pre (ticket segments acknowledged before the response is written, on the capture's stamps), k_in (acknowledged while the burst is being sent), the observed burst.

## What the data show
1. Two regimes, separated by whether any ticket ACK is processed AFTER the write.
   - All ticket ACKs before the write (k_in = 0): the burst is the window of rule (1), `max(10, min(10 + n, 2n))`: 10 for n up to 5 (ECDSA n = 1, ML-DSA-44 n = 4, ML-DSA-65 n = 5: bursts of 10 in 442 of 480, 369 of 480 and 315 of 480 transactions), 12 for n = 6 (ML-DSA-87 at MTU 1 500: 44 of 56 with k_in = 0), 14 for n = 7 (ML-DSA-87 at MTU 1 280: the 8 transactions with every ACK before the write). This is the enlargement seen with the 7-segment ticket and with 6 segments at MTU 1 280: a ticket enlarges the window only from n = 6, which is where 2n passes 10.
   - At least one ticket ACK after the write (k_in >= 1): the burst is larger and nearly constant: 11 for n = 1 (ECDSA: 11 of 11), 13 for n = 4, 5 and 6 (ML-DSA-44 with all four ACKs late: 33 of 33; ML-DSA-65 with two of five: 27 of 27; ML-DSA-87 with all six: 20 of 31, the others from 4 to 12; see the CSV); the late ACKs open the window one by one while the response is being sent.
2. The replay model with the kernel rules (level A, `Simulation/ticket_ackorder_sim.py`, nothing fitted) reproduces both regimes: with the ticket's ACKs late (a delay of 3 ms) it gives a burst of 11 for n = 1 and 13 for n = 4, 5, 6 and 7; with the ACKs early, 10 for n up to 5 and 12 for n = 6. Predicting each transaction from its own order (late if k_in >= 1, else early), 1 466 of 1 747 transactions (83.9 %) have exactly the predicted burst and 1 513 (86.6 %) within one segment; the late regime is predicted in 93 to 100 % of the transactions of every suite at MTU 1 280 and 1 500 except ML-DSA-87 at MTU 1 500 (14 of 51).
3. The misses are mostly "early" transactions with an enlarged burst (ML-DSA-44: 71 of 440; ML-DSA-65: 94 of 409): the capture says the ACKs came before the write but the kernel processed them after it. This is the clock problem already found (the capture's stamps are not the kernel's time; `wp6_clock_check.txt`): the order must be read on the kernel's own clock, which the round-3 probe does (`tcp_ack`).

## Reading
The structure of the replay model (rule (1), `tcp_is_cwnd_limited`, ACKs processed one by one) is right; round 2 failed because its machine times (`c_ack`, `d_tkt`, `p_w`) were fitted per machine on race classes and did not carry over to other cells. If the order of each transaction is MEASURED, the burst follows from the kernel's rules with no fitted parameter. That is the hypothesis M3 of `r3_design.md`; the old data, whose order is blurred by the capture's clock, support it (84 to 87 %) but cannot test it. The intervention with a known delay, read on the kernel's clock, can.
