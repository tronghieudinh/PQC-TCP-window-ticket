# Round 3c: the order of the acknowledgments and the write, moved by delaying the WRITE (23 Nov 2026; criteria C1-C5 APPROVED by the authors on 23 Nov 2026, before any 3c measurement; thresholds still to be derived from the pilot)

Written for: the authors. Follows `r3b_findings.md`. Aim (the authors' rule: the criteria serve the explanation of what was observed): explain WHY the response gets the window that it gets, by predicting, from the times measured with no intervention, how the window moves when the order is moved, on both machines. Time box: two days; if the instrument does not work, stop and report.

## What is established and what is asked
Established (rounds 3 and 3b, 13 198 window events, 2 843 transactions, two machines): the window after the ticket is the kernel's slow-start rule applied to the ticket's acknowledgments processed before the write, the late ones open it while the response is sent, and the burst is `min(cwnd, segments of the response)`. Asked: what sets the ORDER of the acknowledgments' processing and the write, and is it a matter of times that can be moved and predicted? Round 3b moved the acknowledgments (a delay on the pure ACKs) and the order moved on the Arm machine only; on the x86 machine about half of the ticket's acknowledgments are probably carried by the request's data segments and escaped the filter. Round 3c moves the other side of the order: the server's write.

## Intervention
`tls_probe server --pre-write-delay-us D`: after the request has been read, the server waits D microseconds (`clock_nanosleep`) before writing the response. Nothing else changes: the acknowledgments, wherever they travel, arrive when they arrive; a later write lets more of them be processed before it. D = 0 is the unmodified probe's behaviour (the control without the option is the first level). The delay is applied through `trace_run.py` (environment `R3_WRITE_DELAY_US`), the same race design as rounds 3 and 3b (request 25 000 B, RTT 100 ms, 100 Mbit/s, MTU 1 500 and 1 280, ECDSA-P256, ML-DSA-65 and -87, n = 30) with `ticket_ack2.bt`. Levels: GX10 off, 0, 25, 50, 100, 200, 400, 800 us; X250 off, 0, 100, 400, 800, 1 600, 3 200 us (the X250's acknowledgments are processed over a longer stretch: round 3, the median shift of the ticket's ACKs against the write).

## Quantities (all measured on the one clock; nothing fitted)
For every transaction: `t_q` (XQ of the first response segment), the last request data segment's arrival (RX), `p_w` = `t_q` minus that arrival (the time the server takes from the end of the request to the first response segment; the intervention adds D to it), the processing time `AK` of each ticket acknowledgment, `late` = the number of them processed after `t_q`, the window and the burst as in round 3b.

## Prediction (the explanation, made from the D = 0 data alone)
For every transaction measured at D = 0, delaying the write by D moves `t_q` to `t_q + D` and leaves the AK times of its acknowledgments where they are: the predicted number of late acknowledgments at D is the number of its ticket ACKs with `AK > t_q + D`, and the predicted window and burst follow from the same rule as in round 3b applied to the shifted order. The prediction for the late share (and the mean burst) at every D is therefore a function of the D = 0 transactions of the same machine, series and MTU, with no free parameter. The test is whether the transactions measured at D reproduce that prediction.

## Criteria (thresholds: DERIVED where stated, by the rules named; the pilot is the GX10, the test the X250)
- C1 (instrument): the median of `p_w` at each D exceeds the median at D = 0 by D within a tolerance; the 10th-90th percentile spread of `p_w` stays within the spread at D = 0 plus a slope times D. THRESHOLDS derived from the pilot as in `r3b_thresholds.md` (tolerance twice the largest deviation of the pilot's medians, at least 25 us; slope 1.5 times the largest pilot slope).
- C2 (the order moves in the predicted direction; control by the series that cannot move): for each ML-DSA series (suite x MTU) whose late share at D = 0 is at least 50 %, the late share at the largest D is lower by at least half of the smallest pilot decrease; for each series below 50 % at D = 0 the late share at the largest D does not rise by more than the sampling error. THRESHOLDS: the decrease from the pilot (the fraction one half is a judgment, as B4 of round 3b); the sampling error: the 95 % binomial band 1.96 x sqrt(p(1-p)/n) at p = 0.5 and the n of the series (17.9 points for n = 30), a rule with no data in it.
- C3 (the explanation: the late share at each D is predicted by the D = 0 data): for every series and D, the measured late share differs from the prediction by at most the binomial 95 % band at the n of the series (17.9 points for n = 30); the burst itself is not predicted here (it follows from the late acknowledgments by the accounting of C4, which is tested apart). Reported beside it: the error of the null "the late share does not depend on D" (the model of L1 and round 3b's reading before the intervention), so that the gain of the explanation is visible.
- C4 (the rule again, under the new intervention): B1 and B2 with the thresholds already fixed in `r3b_thresholds.md` (99.914 % of the window events, 99.6 % of the transactions): the same accounting must hold when the write is delayed. Inherited, not re-derived.
- C5 (machines): C1 to C4 hold on both machines; the shares of C3 within the band. The X250 is out of sample for every threshold derived from the pilot.
Readings (written beforehand): C1, C2 and C3 met on both machines: the order is set by the times of the acknowledgments and of the write, which can be moved and predicted from the unperturbed times: the window after the ticket is explained by the kernel's rule and by those times (KEPT, levels A and B). C1 and C2 met, C3 not met: the order moves but the unperturbed times do not predict it (the processing times of the acknowledgments themselves depend on the write: IMPROVEMENT ONLY, naming the dependence from the events). C1 not met: the instrument failed, nothing else is concluded. C2 not met with C1 met on a machine: the write does not set the order there; the acknowledgments' own timing does (a finding, with the series that did not move). C4 not met: the accounting of the burst does not survive a delayed write; stop and read the events.

## Fixed on the pilot (23 Nov 2026, before any 3c data of the X250 was opened; see `r3c_thresholds.md`)
1. The shift of the write that the prediction uses is the MEASURED one (median p_w at D minus median p_w at 0), not the nominal D: the pilot showed that the write is later by D plus about 60 us (the timer of `nanosleep`). It is a reading of the instrument (level B), not a fitted parameter.
2. C2: the design asked for series with a late share of at least 50 % at D = 0 and the GX10 has none (0 to 25 %); the criterion is now: a series with at least 10 % at D = 0 must end at the largest D with at most half of it, a series below 10 % must not rise by more than the binomial band (the fractions are judgments). The authors may object before the X250 data are analysed.
3. C1: the tolerance derived from the pilot is 154 us (twice the largest deviation of 77 us, which contains the timer's 60 us); C1 therefore checks that the write moves by about D, not that it moves by exactly D.
