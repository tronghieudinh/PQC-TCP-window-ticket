# Clock-safe repeat of the ticket-acknowledgment traces (option C and criteria K1-K7 APPROVED by the authors on 21 Nov 2026; nothing run yet)

Written for: the authors. Why: `wp6_clock_check.txt` showed that the capture's stamps of the server's own segments are off by about 0.26 ms (the first response segment lies 258 us after the
return of the write that sent it), because the analysis moves them back by half the NOMINAL RTT and netem adds more than that. Ordering client packet / server packet read from the capture is
therefore uncertain by a quarter of a millisecond, which is larger than the 0.049 ms threshold. On the existing race data the share of transactions whose LAST ticket acknowledgment comes before
the write is 84 % with the stamps as they are and 57 % if the stamps are moved by 0.26 ms (`ticket_race.csv`, recomputed 21 Nov); the paper's 90 % (at least one ticket segment acknowledged before
the write) was not recomputed because the per-segment times are not in the file, but it moves the same way.

## What is added (code written 21 Nov, not yet run on any machine)
1. `clock_sync.py` (the procedure). Before each trace it reads bracketed (REALTIME, MONOTONIC) pairs (offset and read error), the kernel's clock state (adjtimex) and the NTP state, and warns if NTP is
   active (slewing moves REALTIME against MONOTONIC by tens of us per second: freq 34.7 ppm was read on the GX10); after the trace it repeats the reading and fails the file if the offset moved by more than
   20 us. `trace_run.py` calls it for every trace (PQC_CLOCK_SYNC=0 disables). On the measurement machine: `sudo timedatectl set-ntp false` before, `true` after.
2. `ticket_xmit.bt` (the probes). One clock for: XQ (segment handed to the device queue, before the netem delay), XS (leaves the qdisc: where tcpdump stamps it), RX (netif_rx: where the arrival stamp is taken)
   and SEND (return of the server's write). The true send time of a server segment is XQ; the delay netem really added is XS - XQ. Used with `PQC_BPF=ticket_xmit.bt`; needs `sudo bpftrace -d` once to check it compiles.
3. To be written after the smoke run shows the real output format (the format of ticket_xmit.bt is the only thing that can differ): `analyze_race_xmit.py` (race data with t_w from XQ and ACK arrival from RX) and
   the extension of `analyze_clock_check.py` for K1-K4.

## Runs (X250, the machine of the first traces; about 1.5 hours; nothing else on the machine)
R0 smoke (n = 3, one cell, 5 minutes): ticket_xmit.bt attaches, XQ/XS/RX/SEND lines appear for the server's port, clock_sync writes a .clock.json that ends `ok`.
R1 part H again (6 files as before: three suites, RTT 30 and 100 ms, arm A, about 25 minutes) with `PQC_BPF=ticket_xmit.bt` PLUS the softirq probes (ticket_softirq.bt with the XQ/XS lines appended), same cells.
R2 the race design again (the first run of 221 transactions: ten networks, suites as in `wp6_analysis_race.txt`) with `PQC_BPF=ticket_xmit.bt`, capture as before (about 40 minutes).

## Criteria (approved 21 Nov 2026)
K1 alignment: the offset between the capture's arrival stamp and RX of the same acknowledgment is within +-20 us of its file's median for at least 95 % of the acknowledgments and at most 50 us for all; and
   `clock_sync --after` ends `ok` for every file.
K2 where tcpdump stamps: capture stamp of a server segment minus the file's offset is within +-20 us of XS of the same segment (matched by sequence number) for at least 95 % of the server segments.
K3 netem (reported, no criterion): XS - XQ per segment by RTT, against the nominal RTT/2 and against `netem_cal_*.json`.
K4 consistency: in at least 99 % of the transactions the XQ of the first response segment is not later than the return of the write that sent it.
K5 the capture puzzle: of the acknowledgments processed after the return of the first response write (the late ones), at least 90 % have RX later than XQ of the first response segment (on the host's own clock they
   did not precede the response). The earlier reading from the capture was that they did.
K6 (race, reported and a rule for the text): with t_w = XQ of the first response segment, report the share of transactions in which at least one ticket segment is acknowledged before the write, overall and among
   those whose burst stayed at ten or less. If both are at least 50 % the sentence of Section 4 stays with the new percentage; if not, it is rewritten (the window then depends on the order after all).
K7 (race, reported): the 0.049 ms rule and its 394 of 399 are computed from client packets only (arrival stamps) and are expected to be unchanged; report them recomputed.

## Options considered
A. No repeat: the supplement states the uncertainty (done). Cost nothing; leaves the 90 % and the puzzle open and a reader can ask why the clock was not fixed. Not recommended.
B. R0 + R1 only (30 minutes of measurement): settles K1-K5, closes the puzzle, does not touch the 90 %.
C. R0 + R1 + R2 (recommended, about 1.5 hours on the X250, free now): also recomputes the 90 %, the only number of the main text that depends on the capture's stamps of server segments.
D. Redo the state trace (931 MB) and the function trace too: not needed, they join by port and acknowledgment number or align through a client packet (no server stamp in their claims).
Risk of C: the 90 % may fall (the stamps put the write too late, so ACKs looked earlier than they were); the text of Section 4 and the supplement then change. That is the purpose of the repeat. Page budget:
main has about 4 lines free; the change replaces a number, or one sentence.

## Outcome of R0-R2 (21 Nov 2026, X250) and the controls it calls for (decision rule written BEFORE the controls run)
Outcome (`wp6_clock_repeat_analysis.txt`): K1 NOT MET (82.8 % within 20 us, 95.7 % within 50 us, largest 279 us; clock_sync ok in 36 of 36 files), K2 NOT MET as registered (79.5 % within 20 us; the spread is narrow, 5th to 95th percentile -26.8 to -9.8 us, around a median of -14.7 us: tcpdump stamps the segment about 15 us before the tracepoint net_dev_start_xmit, so after removing that bias 99.8 % are within 20 us; this is reported as a post hoc reading, the criterion stays unmet), K3 reported (the device adds 153 to 228 us per direction beyond the nominal RTT/2, 185 us at most RTTs), K4 MET (402 of 402), K5 MET (249 of 249), K6 rule of the text: clock-safe share 41 % overall (the capture's t_w, same transactions: 63 %), 79 % among the 115 transactions with a burst of ten or less, so the rule rewrites the sentence; K7: 166 of 194 ML-DSA transactions (first run 132 of 134).
The K6 and K7 numbers cannot be read as an effect of the clock yet: R2 does not reproduce the first run in the capture alone. In the first run ECDSA-P256 had a burst of ten in every cell (0 of 73 above ten) and ML-DSA-44 at 5 ms ten; in R2 43 of 100 ECDSA-P256 transactions are above ten, and ML-DSA-44 at 5 ms is 13. Two differences between the runs can explain it: the instruments (R2 has four bpftrace probes on every packet, the first run had capture and tcp_probe only; the mechanism is sensitive to microseconds) or the state of the machine on the day. Controls (about 15 minutes, `run_clock_repeat.sh ctrl`): C1 = the race design with the instruments of the first run (capture and tcp_probe, no bpftrace, no clock_sync); C2 = R2 again.
Decision rule: compare the bursts per cell with `compare_race_bursts.py`. If C1 reproduces the first run (ECDSA-P256 at ten in at least 90 % of its transactions, ML-DSA-44 at ten at 5 ms) and C2 reproduces R2, the bpftrace probes perturb the timing: K6 and K7 of R2 are then not interpretable, the probe set has to be lightened (XQ and RX only) and R2 repeated. If C1 also shows the pattern of R2, the machine on the day differs from that of the first run: the first run's numbers (221, 90 %, 132 of 134) are replaced in the paper by those of a complete repeat, and the criteria K6 and K7 are read on C1 and R2 together. If C2 differs from R2, the run-to-run variation is larger than the effects tested and the paper says so.

## Outcome of the controls C1 and C2 (21 Nov 2026, 16:14) and the next step (rule written BEFORE the tuned runs)
`compare_race_bursts.py`: share of resumed transactions with a burst above ten, ECDSA-P256 / ML-DSA-44 / ML-DSA-65: first run 0/73, 40/75, 54/73; R2 43/100, 65/100, 71/100; C1 (the instruments of the first run) 48/94, 61/91, 69/90; C2 (R2 again) 45/100, 67/100, 76/100. C1 and C2 reproduce R2, not the first run: the bpftrace probes are not what changed the timing, and the run-to-run variation is small. By the decision rule above, the machine on the day differs from that of the first run.
A difference found afterwards in the scripts, not a result: the scripts of the first traces (`run_wp6b.sh`, `run_wp6c.sh`, `run_wp6d.sh`, `run_wp6f.sh`, `run_wp6h.sh`) call `host_tune.sh on` (performance governor, no turbo, checked before the run) and `run_clock_repeat.sh` did not, so R0-R2, C1 and C2 ran with the CPU clock as the system leaves it. The mechanism is sensitive to microseconds, so a changing CPU frequency is the first suspect. This is a hypothesis, and the repeat below tests it. `clock_sync.py` now records the governor, no_turbo and the frequencies, and warns when the clock is not fixed; `run_clock_repeat.sh` fixes it by default (PQC_NO_TUNE=1 keeps the old behaviour); the new step `tuned` runs C1, R1 and R2 with the clock fixed (output directories end in _t, about 15 minutes).
T1 (reproduction, decided before the run): C1 with the clock fixed gives a burst of ten in at least 90 % of the ECDSA-P256 transactions (first run 73 of 73) and a median burst of ten for ML-DSA-44 at RTT 5 ms (first run: ten in 5 of 5).
If T1 is met: the CPU clock state explains the difference; R1 and R2 with the clock fixed are the clock-safe repeat proper, K1-K7 are evaluated on them (their results replace those of the untuned runs, which are kept and reported as a finding: the ordering at the server depends on the CPU frequency state), and the numbers of Section 4 and the supplement that came from the first run stay unless K6 and K7 on the fixed-clock runs say otherwise.
If T1 is not met: something else differs from the first run (kernel command line, services, the build of tls_probe or of OpenSSL, certificates); the differences are listed and K6 and K7 stay uninterpretable; the paper then says what the data cannot separate.

## C. Which factor of the CPU clock matters (APPROVED by the authors on 21 Nov 2026; criteria written before the runs)
Question: the race design differs between the CPU clock fixed (performance governor, turbo off: ECDSA-P256 above ten in 0 of 100) and as the system leaves it (governor ondemand, turbo on: 43 to 48 of 100). Which of the two settings carries the difference? On the X250, R2 only, two variants (`sudo bash run_clock_repeat.sh isolA`, `... isolB`, about 6 minutes each, directories `_isolA` and `_isolB`; each leaves the machine as it found it):
A: performance governor, turbo ON. B: the system's own governor (ondemand), turbo OFF.
Prediction (made before the runs): the governor carries it. A looks like the fixed clock (ECDSA-P256 above ten in at most 10 of 100 transactions); B looks like the clock as left (at least 25 of 100). Reading: A fixed-like and B left-like: the governor carries it and turbo does not matter; A left-like and B fixed-like: turbo carries it; both fixed-like or both left-like or in between: neither setting alone explains it (the interplay, or the frequency actually reached, matters) and the paper says only that the state of the CPU clock matters. The best threshold of the 0.049 ms rule on ML-DSA at MTU below 9 000 and the K4, K5 and K6 numbers are reported for each variant, no criterion.
