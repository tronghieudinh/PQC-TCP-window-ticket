# G23 on the GX10 (Arm), 22 Nov 2026: what the data say (run 20261122-141612-gx10-g23-gx10; analysis `g23_gx10_analysis.txt`)

Measured: 105 calls, 0 failed, n = 30 per cell, CPU clock fixed, server on CPU 5 and client on 6 and 8 (X925), load 0.4 to 1.0 from other users; all cells have 30 valid transactions; the bytes equal the calibration (0 cells off by more than 16 B). Design and bands as registered (commit 9fc59f9).

## Registered readings (not exploratory)
- Stage T (the ticket data from 0 to 18 segments with the suite, the ClientHello, the client's window and the request unchanged; 100 cells): the latency follows `inflight`, the model of L1 (the tickets share the server's window of ten with the response), on ALL cells where the two predictions differ, and `acked` (rule (1) on the ticket flight) on none. The response size at which the first extra round trip appears equals the model's in all 10 series (0 to 4 tickets, two suites). So P2 (ticket size sets the server's window) is NOT supported at a small request; the enlargement seen in L1 and in the GX10 exploratory runs is not a property of the ticket size alone. P1 holds on a third library.
- C: rule (1) wrong on 0 of 32 (band 0-2); the loosened rule and textbook slow start wrong on all their discriminating cells (3 of 3, 13 of 13). D: `early` wrong on 0 of the 35 cells where it differs from `joined` (band 0-3), on 6 of 85 overall (band 0-14). R: `early` wrong on 6 of 40 (ABOVE the band 0-3). O (OpenSSL control, same session): wrong on 0 of 20 (band 0-2): the session is sound.
- Direction: the model is too fast by more than half an RTT on 0 cells of C, D and R (157 cells, band 0-1), and all 12 wrong cells are too slow (by 16 to 197 ms): the one-sided error of L1 holds on a third library and on the Arm machine.

## What the wrong cells have in common (diagnosis, registered as a diagnosis)
All 12 have a LARGE request (14 to 21 KB, 10 to 15 segments, a request flight that needs another round trip). In that branch of the model the ticket has been acknowledged before the response leaves, and the model leaves the server's window at ten (Net.srv_cwnd_acked = 0): there is the error.

## Exploratory (written after the GX10 data were seen; `gnutls_explore.py`; NOT a test)
H_ack: in that branch the server's window is rule (1) applied to the ticket flight (14, 18, 22 segments for ML-DSA-44, -65, -87). On the GX10 cells it makes 11 of the 12 wrong cells right (D 6 to 3 wrong, R 6 to 1) but breaks 3 cells of ML-DSA-87 (k = 19 to 21: the model then too fast; the measured window is about 18, rule (1) gives 22: the window after a 12-segment ticket is smaller than rule (1)). On the OpenSSL random draws of L1 (G19, four runs) H_ack changes no cell (tickets of about 5 segments: rule (1) gives 10), so the 5 wrong cells there stay unexplained. H_ack is a PARTIAL law. Predictions for the X250 cells of G23 with both models are written in `g23_explore_predictions_{d,r}.csv` and committed before the X250 data are looked at: the X250 data are then a test of H_ack on another machine (same cells, so not new cells).
Open for round 3: the window after a ticket of 12 segments (about 18, not 22); why OpenSSL's small tickets also enlarge the window in some cells; whether the ACK order of the microsecond effect (the 0.049 ms proxy) decides the branch.

# Addendum: the X250 (x86), same day (run 20261122-145057-X250-g23-X250; analysis `g23_x250_analysis.txt`; 105 calls, 0 failed, n = 30, load not recorded here)
- Registered readings, the same as on the GX10: stage T follows `inflight` on all 100 cells (0 on `acked`), the extra round trip appears at the same response size as the model's in all 10 series; C: rule (1) wrong on 0 of 32 and the alternatives refuted on all discriminating cells; D: `early` wrong on 0 of 35 differing cells, 7 of 85 overall; R: 7 of 40 wrong (ABOVE the band 0-3); O: 0 of 20 (session sound); too fast by half an RTT on 0 cells, all 14 wrong cells too slow.
- Across the machines: of 257 cells measured on both, the measured excess differs by more than half an RTT on 2; the 12 wrong cells of the GX10 are wrong on the X250 too (14 there: two more, ML-DSA-65 request 19 460 B k = 19, and ECDSA-P256 request 13 008 B at RTT 150 ms). So the effect does not depend on the machine at this resolution: P3 (the ACK order decides, machine by machine) is not supported by T, and the wrong cells are cells of the cell, not of the machine.
- H_ack on the X250 (predictions registered before the data were looked at, same cells as on the GX10): D 7 wrong with the baseline, 4 with H_ack (6 fixed, 3 broken: the same ML-DSA-87 k = 19 to 21; 1 wrong with both); R 7 wrong, 2 with H_ack (5 fixed, 0 broken, 2 wrong with both: ML-DSA-44 request 15 939 B, and the ECDSA-P256 cell above, a ticket of 2 segments that rule (1) cannot enlarge). The same picture as on the GX10: H_ack is a partial law (it explains the cells of large tickets, not the cells of small ones, and over-enlarges the window after a ticket of 12 segments).
- Consequence for the text of L1 (the authors decide): the third library confirms the model of L1 on 100 cells of the ticket sweep and on the cells where `early` and `joined` differ, and confirms the one-sided error (0 too fast in 314 cell-runs of C, D, R on two machines); where the model is wrong the cause is not the size of the ticket nor the machine but the request flight that needs another round trip; the window after the acknowledged ticket stays open.
- The declared repeat on the X250 is not needed for the conclusions above (2 of 257 cells differ between two different machines); it can be run later as the noise floor.
