# Code, data and predictions for "How Linux Grows the TCP Window After a Short Flight, and What It Costs Post-Quantum TLS in Round Trips"

Repeatable paper (ACM SIGCOMM Computer Communication Review); its appendix says how to repeat the key results. Authors: Trong-Hieu Dinh (Banking Academy of Vietnam), <hieudt@hvnh.edu.vn>; Trong-Minh Hoang (Posts and Telecommunications Institute of Technology, corresponding author), <hoangtrongminh@ptit.edu.vn>.

The paper measures how Linux grows the TCP congestion window after a short flight (plain TCP, 13 configurations, four kernels, three CPUs), refutes with predictions fixed in advance a larger-window explanation of a model error, and shows with an intervention that the TLS session ticket, a write of its own, enlarges the server's window through acknowledgments that the kernel processes while the sender is window-limited. It is a companion of the study archived in `pqc-financial-transport` (Zenodo concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23839449), whose per-segment transport model it uses (`Simulation/pqc_sim.py`).

## Layout (the paths are those of the working repository, so that no script needs another path)
- `paper2_short/CCR_repeatable/`: the manuscript (`main.tex`, `refs.bib`; ACM `acmart`, build with `latexmk -pdf main.tex`) and its supplement (`supplement_ack.tex`). `paper2_short/CCR/` (l1-v1.0) and `paper2_short/CCR_v3/` (l1-v1.1 and l1-v1.2) are earlier versions, kept for the record; they read the current files of `paper2_short/Latex/`, so rebuild them from their tags.
- `paper2_short/Latex/`: `make_assets_short.py` (figures and `numbers_short.tex` from the data), the figures and the numbers as used in the paper.
- `Simulation/`: the transport model (`pqc_sim.py`) and its loss extension.
- `Measurements/testbed/`: the testbed (`tbnet.py`, `run_slack.py`, `run_testbed.py`, `trace_run.py`), the predictions scripts (`*_predict.py`), one analysis script per experiment (`analyze_*.py`) and the bpf probes (`*.bt`).
- `Measurements/testbed/results/`: the predictions fixed before each measurement (`slack_predictions.csv`, `nst_predictions*.csv`, `wp6_pred_*`), the data of the main machine, the per-acknowledgment tables (`ticket_state.csv`, `ticket_backlog.csv`, `ticket_race.csv`) and the analysis outputs (`wp6_analysis_*.txt`, `*_analysis.txt`); four working notes of the replication are in Vietnamese (`gx10_*_phan_tich_*.txt`, `paper2_short/results/window_rule_across_machines_20261115.txt`: tables of numbers).
- `data/<machine>/<kernel>/<stage>/`: raw files of the other machines (plain-TCP window test with OpenSSL 3.5.4 on every machine and kernel; ticket tests on the Arm machine), as written by the measurement scripts.
- `Measurements/scenarios/shared/`: the scenarios of the replications and robustness tests (G10 Go `crypto/tls`, G11 BBR and fq, G12 new RTTs and request sizes, G13 real link, G14 BBR and delayed acknowledgments, G16 loss, G17 background load, G18 Go pipelined variant, G19 random design and G19b, G20 borderline cells, G21 tails), with `run.sh`, `scenario.json` and `README.md` each. Raw data of these runs are in `Measurements/testbed/results/g9/`, `g10/` to `g21/` and, for the runs of 21 November 2026 on the Arm machine, the OptiPlex and the X250, in `data/<machine>/scenarios/`.
- For new measurements (level 3 of the appendix): `Measurements/testbed/setup_host.sh` (builds OpenSSL 3.5 and the probes, then runs `Measurements/certs.sh`), the C sources, `Measurements/scenarios/common/` and the scenarios G9, G11 and G19, and `Measurements/testbed/run_g24.sh`. The scenario scripts require the prediction files to be committed in git.
- `tools/raw_traces.sh`: re-analysis of the raw traces (see below).
- `reproduce_l1.sh`: regenerates the figures and the numbers from the data, checks them against the shipped `numbers_short.tex` (`numbers check OK` or `numbers DIFF`) and builds the PDF.

## Numbers computed in this repository
Every number of the paper is computed by `paper2_short/Latex/make_assets_short.py` from the files here, including the pre-registered test with certificates of different algorithms (`analyze_wp6.py`, `wp6_pred_clientalg.csv`, `resume_summary_wp6_clientalg_*.csv`) and the MTU 9 000 tests (`analyze_mtu9000_records.py`, `analyze_rwnd.py`, `resume_summary_wp6_mtu9000_*.csv`, `rwnd_default/`, `rwnd_large/`). The 240-cell scan and the server-authentication-only test are reported in `wp6_analysis_gen.txt` and `wp6_analysis_servonly.txt`.

## Raw traces and what is not here
The raw kernel traces and packet captures of the ticket tests (pcap, ftrace text, bpftrace logs; 98 MB compressed) are archived in a separate Zenodo record, doi:10.5281/zenodo.24384873; the raw files of the clock-safe repeat of the ticket traces (21 November 2026, eight archives, 82 MB, with `Measurements/testbed/results/RAW_TRACES_CLOCK_REPEAT.md` and their checksums) are a later version of that record, doi:10.5281/zenodo.24419058, which also holds the ten archives of the exploratory runs and of rounds 3, 3b, 3c and 3d of 22-23 November 2026 (`Measurements/testbed/results/RAW_TRACES_ROUNDS_R3.md`, 291 MB with checksums). The analyses that read them (`analyze_state.py`, `analyze_ftrace.py`, `analyze_softirq_posthoc.py`, `analyze_backlog.py`, `analyze_notkt.py`, `analyze_race2.py`, `analyze_ticket_race.py`) are included with their outputs. `bash tools/raw_traces.sh verify DIR` re-runs them on the unpacked traces (`DIR` holds the `trace_*` directories) and compares each output with the shipped one; `Measurements/testbed/results/RAW_TRACES_VERIFICATION_20261120.md` records the result of that check (every number reproduced). Test private keys are not distributed.
